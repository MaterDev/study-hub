Why AI Helps Some People and Hurts Others

A five-lesson module on individual differences in generative-AI effectiveness — and what to do about them.

BY THE END OF THIS MODULE YOU CAN…
  • Explain why identical AI tools produce very different outcomes for different people.
  • Spot when a task sits outside the AI's "jagged frontier" — and why that's hard to see.
  • Distinguish assisted performance from independent capability, and why AI can raise one while lowering the other.
  • Name the different risks for novices (upskilling inhibition) and experts (deskilling).
  • Apply three design levers to your own work so productivity gains don't cost you skill.
  • Judge how strong each piece of evidence actually is.

1 The big picture

Same tool in, very different results out. The source paper's central claim: that variance is driven by individual differences — and using the tool feeds back to change those differences over time.

3 Key terms

Compression effectAI lifts lower performers the most, narrowing the gap with top performers.
Jagged frontierThe irregular boundary between tasks AI does well and tasks where it quietly fails.
Epistemic calibrationHow well your confidence matches your real knowledge — and the AI's real output quality.
Cognitive offloadingMoving mental work onto an external tool. Generative AI offloads generation itself.
DeskillingLosing a skill you already had, through disuse (the expert risk).
Upskilling inhibitionA skill never forming because the tool did the formative work (the novice risk).
Preserved generationDesigning AI use so you make the first attempt, and AI shapes or checks it.
Two horizonsJudging AI use by today's task and the capability you'll need without the tool.
